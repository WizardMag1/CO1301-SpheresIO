Introduction
The commercial video games industry has been around since the 1970s
Nowadays, it is a global, multi-billion-dollar industry
Combines several aspects of other industries such as creativity, technology and business strategy
This industry includes key players such as developers/studios, publishers, console manufacturers and retailers.
All these involved parties share a common goal: 
The production of games that sell in huge quantities, for players on serval platforms, such as mobile, home consoles, handheld  consoles, PCs, Web, VR Headsets etc.
--- slide 5 ---
Players Involved: Game Manufacturers 
What are they?
Companies that design, produce and maintain gaming hardware planforms
Provide the ecosystems where games are played
What is their role?
Define technical standards and performance capabilities for games
Offer development kits (SDKs) and publishing support to studios
Operate digital marketplaces (e.g. PlayStation Store XBOX Store, etc)
--- slide 6 ---
Players Involved: Game Developers/Studios 
The games industry isn’t one-size-fits-all and it includes many types of studios that differ in terms of scale, funding and creativity control.
AAA Studios:  Large-scale studios with major funding and big teams. They focus on high production values and global releases (e.g., Riot Games – League of Legends).
AA Studios: Medium-scale studios with moderate budget (e.g. Krafton’s PUBG Studios – PUBG: Battlegrounds)
Indie Studios:  Small, independent teams or solo developers. Minimal funding – typically self-funded (e.g. Extremely OK Games – Tower Fall)
Outsourcing &amp; Support Studios: Provide art, animation or other important services to other studios (e.g. Room 8 studio)
--- slide 7 ---
Players Involved: Publishers
What are they?
Companies that fund, market and distribute game projects
Act as the bridge between developers and the marketplace
Can sometimes own game studios (e.g. Ubisoft, EA, Epic Games, Riot, Nintendo, Activision Blizzard, etc)
What is their role?
Financing a game (provide money for production and marketing)
Project Management (Oversee milestones and quality standards)
Marketing (Promote the game to media)
Sometimes handle Distribution (retail and logistics)
--- slide 8 ---
Players Involved - Retailers
Sometimes publishers are not handing the retail of their games and retailers are becoming responsible for that
What are they?
Businesses that sell games directly to the consumers
Can be physical stores (e.g. GameStop, Best Buy) or digital platforms (e.g. Stram, Nintendo eShop)
Act as the final link in the commercial games supply chain
What is their role?
Provide sales, promotion and discoverability for games
Handle transactions, pre-orders and pricing
Gather sales data and player trends which are valuable for future projects.

Where do Game Ideas Come from?
Game ideas can come from anywhere
A memory, a mechanic, a book, a film, a dream, a myth, pure imagination…
Many iconic games began with a simple question or an experiment:
What if we let players build their own world? – Minecraft
What if stealth was a mechanic? – Metal Gear Solid
Typically, an original concept can be created by a developer
However, it is also very common for a game concept to be inspired by business decisions:
A publishing company deciding to make a sequel
A publishing company making a deal with a movie studio (movie-based game)
--- slide 11 ---
The Game Development Process
A developer with a game concept tries to get a publisher to fund the developmentORA publisher interested in developing a game and tries to get a developer
In either case, a publisher funds a developer to create a game
The publisher markets and distributes the finished game
The game is sold by retailers
Revenue is shared by all parties involved
--- slide 12 ---
The Game Development Process
However….. This is not always the case!
There are some cases where:
A cash-rich development company can fund themselves
A publisher can own a game studio and fund its own development team
Console manufacturers sometimes act as publishers, and can own their own game  studio(s)

--- slide 14 ---
Phases of Commercial Games Development
Most commercial game development projects are broken down into four phases:
Concept Phase
Pre-production Phase
Production Phase
Post-production Phase
--- slide 15 ---
Concept Phase
The phase were the game idea is conceptualized
The team agrees on the game’s mechanics, visual style, game play etc
The team produces a concept document that communicates the vison of the  game. 
Answers to the main questions that will asked be asked by potential publishers.
If a publisher agrees to fund the development of the game, the project is green-lighted.
To fund the game an important step is for the team to agree on “milestones” with the publisher
Typically, in each milestone the developers submit the product to the publisher
…and typically, the team is paid by the “milestones” reached.
If funding is secured, then the team proceeds to the Pre-Production phase
--- slide 16 ---
Pre-Production Phase
In this phase the Visual elements (e.g. look and feel), technical details (e.g. game engine), and story are specified.
A playable demo of the game is created to demonstrate the eventual game experience.
A game design document is produced to prepare for full scale production (more about
GDD in next week’s lecture)
Then, all the management of the development process, including hiring staff and assignment of roles and responsibilities, is specified.
--- slide 17 ---
Production Phase
In this phase the game design document is followed by the team, and the game is developed, in 2 phases (alpha and beta). 
This phase usually takes about 50% of the entire project life-cycle.
Alpha Stage: The game reaches this stage when all its features and assets are in place, although some assets may not be in their final versions.
In this stage, bugs are identified, and all  final assets are added.
Beta Stage: The games goes to beta once all assets, objects, environments etc are completed and all bugs are fixed.
Following Beta, the game is opened up to players for testing, finding and fixing bugs, and game-play evaluation.
Finally, the Gold Master disc is created, sent for reproduction, and copies distributed to
retailers.
--- slide 18 ---
Post-Production Phase
Once a game is released, the post production phase begins.
This phase includes maintenance in the form of patches.
DLCs can also be introduced in the game in this phase.

The Development Team and Roles
Typically, the development team is separated into four sub-groups:
Management Group – responsible for managing the game progress
Programmers – responsible for coding the game
Artists – responsible for the art of the game
Designers – responsible for gameplay
The size of a each development team can  vary depending of the project size and the stage of  development
Teams can have anywhere from 2 to 100 members.

--- slide 22 ---
Game Programmers
Game programmers are the people responsible for implementing the mechanics and logic to the game.
To implement games, they use game specific tools and API’s (Application Programmer Interfaces), e.g. DirectX, Unity or Unreal Engine.
TL-Engine is an example of a games API.
…and to code the game they usually use C++, C# and other scripting languages (Lua and python) using a standard development environment like Microsoft Visual Studio.
With that said...
Don’t consider games development as a different process from the development of other programs and software. 
Although it is more creative, at its core it is an ordinary software engineering discipline.
--- slide 23 ---
Game Artists
Game Artists are the people responsible for implementing the models and the art of the game.
Game artists 
Realize the concept illustrations using 3D modelling tools (e.g. Blender, or Maya).
Create animations for the characters  and objects in the game.
And create 2D graphics if necessary.
However,
3D modelling tools can also leverage programming, using “scripts".
Scripts are used to help "export" models into game engines, and also create complex animations.
--- slide 24 ---
Artists and Programmers
The interaction between artists and programmers  is COMPLEX
…and each side must understand the  other’s work to a degree.
This aspect of games development can be a source  of inspiration or cause great friction.
For example, artists develop super realistic models something that makes the game very resource intensive.
Those who understand the processes are very valuable assets on development teams.
However, there are VERY, VERY FEW people who are  commercially skilled in both disciplines.
--- slide 25 ---
Game Designers
Game Designers are professionals who create the rules, systems, and experiences that define how a game is played.
They are responsible for: 
Developing the game objectives and challenges
Write and maintaining the Game Design Document
Collaborate with artists and programmers to align their visions
There are several types of Game Designers:
Gameplay Designers: Focus on the design of core game mechanics and interactions
Level Designers: Focus on creating the environment design, pacing and game flow
Narrative Designers: Focus on developing storylines, dialogues and emotional tone
System Designers: Focus on balancing game progress, economy and reward systems
…among other types
--- slide 26 ---
Management - Game Producer
The game management team (producers) are responsible for managing and assessing the progress made on the game, and ensure that all milestones set at the concept phase are met.
In the industry their position can be found with several names such as:
Project managers
Project leaders
Creative directors
Etc..
--- slide 27 ---
Other Roles
On top of these roles, depending on the needs of the game, other roles can also exist…
Games, especially AAA games they often require several other components in order to be developed.
Some examples include:
Sound effects and speech
Motion capture
Front-end menu programming and artwork
Network and other hardware support
Security
Story text and localisation
Instruction manual
As a result, people from other specializations might also be members of a game development team
