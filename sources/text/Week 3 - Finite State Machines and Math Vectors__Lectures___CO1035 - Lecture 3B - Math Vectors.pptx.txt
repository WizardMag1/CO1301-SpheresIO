Right-angle triangles
One of the properties of right-angle triangles is Pythagoras’ Theorem.
Pythagoras' Theorem: the length of the longest side of a right-angled triangle is equal to the square root of the sum of the squares of the other two sides.
The longest side of a right-angled triangle is called the ‘hypotenuse’.
--- slide 6 ---
SCALARS AND VECTORS
 Scalar: a physical quantity that is completely described by its magnitude
 (e.g.) Distance, Volume, Time, Temperature, Mass, etc.
 Vectors: a physical quantity that is fully described by its magnitude and its direction
 (e.g.) Displacement, Force, Velocity, etc.
--- slide 7 ---
coordinates
A coordinate represents a point in space.
A point in 2D space is represented using two coordinates (x, y) e.g. (1,4)
In 3D a point is represented using three coordinates (x,  y, z) e.g. (5,3,9)
In 3D - This is called Cartesian Geometry.

LOCAL VS GLOBALCOORDINATES
The world (or screen) has its x, y, z axes and its own origin (Global Coordinates)
Every object in the world also has its own origin and x, y and z axes (Local Coordinates)

VECTORS
 Vectors are entities with magnitude and direction.
 We visualize vectors by drawing a line with an arrowhead at one end.
 The length of the line represents the magnitude of the vector.
 And the arrowhead indicates the vector’s direction

--- slide 12 ---
VECTOR BETWEEN TWO POINTS
 The vector between two points can be calculated by subtracting one point from the other:
 Subtract the initial x value from the final x value to produce one new x; 
 Subtract the initial y value from the final y value to produce one new y;
 Subtract the initial z value from the final z value to produce one new z;

Vector direction
 Notice how the values in the vector are found by subtracting the source point coordinates from the destination point coordinates. 
 This means that the direction of the vector is important.
 And that the vector in the reverse direction from P2 to P1 is not the same as the vector from P1 to P2.

Vectors (CONT.)
Vectors are location independent
The vector is the same no matter what its coordinates are.
Two vectors with the same magnitude  and direction are equal regardless of  their location
--- slide 17 ---
Vector Addition
Two vectors v and w are added by placing the beginning of w at the end of v.
--- slide 18 ---
VECTOR ADDITION (CONT.)
However, since vectors are independent of the coordinates, the addition of 2 vectors will lead to the same result (e.g.)
v(0, 1) + w(2, 3) = (0+2, 1+3) = (2, 4)
w(2, 3) + v(0, 1) = (2+0, 3+1) = (2, 4)
v + w = w + v
--- slide 19 ---
Vector Subtraction
Subtraction works in a similar way to addition. 
The direction in which the subtracted vector points is effectively reversed.
v(3, 3) - w(1, 2) = (3 -1, 3 -2) = (2, 1)
Note: v(3, 3) - w(1, 2) = (2, 1) is not equals w(2, 1) - v(3, 3) = (-1, -2)

Scalar Multiplication
Direction vectors are always of length 1 (the unit length).
 To describe an actual movement, you combine the direction vector with a scalar.
The scalar is the distance to be moved.
Imagine that you want to move something by a given distance in a particular direction. We multiply the direction vector by the distance we want to move.
For example, to move an object 4 units using  the normalised vector ( 0, 1, 0 ), the movement  vector becomes ( 0, 4, 0 )

Summary
 A scalar has magnitude; a vector has magnitude and direction.
The vector between two points can be calculated by subtracting one point from the other.
The length of a vector can be calculated using Pythagoras’ theorem.
Vectors can be added and subtracted.
Unit vector is a vector of length one (a normalized vector).
A direction vector is a unit vector used to represent the direction of a  vector.
A vector can be scaled through scaler multiplication.
